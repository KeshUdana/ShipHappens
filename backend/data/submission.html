Section 01
Project Overview

Project Name & One-Line Pitch

PaperForge — an AI-powered exam paper generator that turns 3–5 uploaded A-Level English papers into a brand-new, constraint-matched exam paper in under 90 seconds, giving teachers back hours of drafting time every week.

Summary

A-Level English teachers spend 4–8 hours manually drafting each practice paper — cross-checking mark allocations, question variety, difficulty balance, and syllabus alignment, all under the time pressure of exam season. PaperForge solves this by extracting the structural DNA of a set of uploaded papers into a precise "blueprint" (sections, marks, question types), then instructing Google Gemini to generate a fresh paper that satisfies every constraint exactly. The output appears live in the browser via streaming, is fully editable at the question level, pairs with an automatically generated answer key and marking scheme, and exports to a print-ready PDF. The primary users are A-Level and O-Level English teachers at tutoring centres and secondary schools, particularly across the UK and Southeast Asia where Cambridge qualifications dominate.

Submission Details

Track: Gemini

Track technology integration: Google Gemini is the core engine. Three distinct Gemini capabilities are used: gemini-2.5-pro / gemini-2.5-flash for structured paper generation and blueprint extraction (Gemini Files API + response_schema), gemini-2.5-flash for per-question regeneration and answer key generation, and text-embedding-004 for embedding-based duplicate content detection.

Section 02
Problem Statement

The Problem

A-Level and O-Level English teachers at tutoring centres and secondary schools spend 4–8 hours drafting a single practice exam paper. This is not a one-off effort — teachers need a continuous supply of fresh papers, typically 1–4 new papers per exam season per subject, to ensure students are not repeating questions they have already seen. Each paper must simultaneously maintain correct total marks, balance question types across sections (MCQ, structured response, essays), match the appropriate reading level and passage length, and align to the Cambridge Assessment International Education syllabus.

If left unsolved, teachers either produce fewer practice papers — directly harming student outcomes at a high-stakes stage — or burn out from the drafting workload on top of their teaching, marking, and administrative duties.

Why It Matters

The problem peaks twice a year: April–May and October–November, when A-Level exam seasons align. During these windows, the demand for practice materials spikes exactly when teachers have the least time to produce them.

Current workarounds all fall short:

  • Hand-editing old papers — slow, risks students recognising questions, and requires full re-typesetting for changed mark allocations.
  • Third-party question banks (e.g., Hodder Education, licensed Cambridge packs) — expensive (£200–500/year per teacher), inflexible, and not customisable to a school's specific teaching style or local cultural context.
  • Generic AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini web) — require the teacher to be skilled at prompt engineering, produce unstructured output, perform no marks accounting, and cannot export a formatted paper.

Root Cause

There is no tool that understands the structural DNA of an exam paper — the precise distribution of marks, question types, instruction styles, reading passage requirements, and section constraints — and can reproduce that structure with entirely fresh content at machine speed. The problem is not that AI cannot write exam questions. The problem is that nobody has built the pipeline that extracts constraints from real papers, enforces them during generation, validates the output, and delivers it in a form teachers can actually use.

Section 03
Proposed Solution

What the Product Does

PaperForge is a browser-based tool with a four-step wizard: Upload → Blueprint Review → Editable Paper → Export.

The teacher drops 3–5 existing A-Level papers onto the upload page. Each PDF is stored in Supabase Storage and uploaded to the Gemini Files API, where it is cached for 48 hours. The teacher clicks "Extract Blueprint" — Gemini analyses all papers simultaneously and returns a BlueprintSchema: a structured JSON object encoding the exact section layout, marks per section, marks per question, question types (MCQ, structured, essay), context-passage requirements, and typical instruction phrasing. The teacher reviews the blueprint and can adjust any field before proceeding.

Clicking "Generate Paper" opens a Server-Sent Events stream. Gemini generates a full paper constrained by the blueprint — the backend post-validates that marks totals are within 10% of the target and retries if not. Section completion events fire in real time so the teacher sees the paper building out live rather than staring at a spinner. The result is a fully editable paper in the browser. The teacher can edit any question inline, regenerate a single question with an optional nudge instruction, or regenerate an entire section. A parallel Gemini call generates a complete answer key and marking scheme on demand. A duplicate-detection check (embedding cosine similarity against the source PDFs) flags any generated question that is too close to source content. When satisfied, the teacher exports to a print-ready PDF.

Section 04
Functional Requirements
Must Have
  • The system shall accept 3–5 PDF uploads per session and store them in Supabase Storage.
  • The system shall upload each PDF to the Gemini Files API and cache the file URI to avoid re-upload on retry.
  • The system shall extract a BlueprintSchema — sections, marks totals, question types, instruction phrasing — from the uploaded PDFs via a single Gemini call.
  • The system shall generate a full exam paper constrained by the blueprint, with marks drift ≤10% from the target total.
  • The system shall stream generation progress to the frontend via Server-Sent Events, emitting section-completion events in real time.
  • The system shall display the generated paper with inline text editing on any question field.
  • The system shall support per-question regeneration with an optional teacher nudge string.
  • The system shall export the current paper state to a print-ready HTML/PDF via a Jinja2 template.
Should Have
  • The system should generate a complete answer key and marking scheme for any paper on demand.
  • The system should assign a difficulty score (1–5) to every question during generation.
  • The system should check generated questions against source PDFs for duplicate content using embedding cosine similarity.
  • The system should support per-section regeneration (replacing all questions in one section).
  • The system should fall back from gemini-2.5-pro to gemini-2.5-flash automatically on quota exhaustion.
  • The system should record token usage and estimated cost (USD) for every Gemini call in api_usage_log.
Could Have / Won't Have (This Build)
  • User authentication and persistent paper library (out of scope — anonymous sessions only).
  • Multi-subject support beyond A-Level English (out of scope — prompt re-engineering required).
  • LMS export to Google Classroom or Canvas (post-launch).
  • Collaborative editing (multiple teachers on one paper simultaneously).
  • Cost/token dashboard in the UI (logged to DB; UI display is post-MVP).
  • Server-side headless PDF renderer (browser print dialog is sufficient for MVP).

Section 05
Non-Functional Requirements

Performance

  • Blueprint extraction from 5 PDFs: ≤15 seconds
  • Paper generation (first SSE event): ≤5 seconds; complete paper: ≤90 seconds
  • Per-question regeneration: ≤10 seconds
  • Answer key generation: ≤20 seconds
  • PDF export render: ≤2 seconds

Reliability & Error Handling

  • All Gemini calls wrapped in a retry loop (max 3 attempts, exponential back-off)
  • Quota exhaustion (HTTP 429): automatic model fallback from gemini-2.5-pro → gemini-2.5-flash
  • Post-validation on every AI response; marks drift >10% triggers a retry rather than returning bad output
  • Corrupted or too-short PDFs rejected at upload with a clear user-facing message
  • Mid-stream Gemini errors emit a single {event:"error"} SSE message and close the connection cleanly

Usability

  • Wizard-style navigation with clear step indicators
  • Mobile-responsive layout (shadcn/ui + Tailwind CSS)
  • SSE progress events eliminate the blank-spinner problem during long generation
  • Inline editing requires no modal or separate save step
  • Accessible colour contrast (WCAG AA) in the default theme

Scalability

  • FastAPI backend is stateless; session state lives in Supabase (scales horizontally)
  • Gemini Files API 48-hour cache eliminates repeat-upload token cost as teacher iterates
  • pgvector extension installed and question_embedding table ready for a cross-session question bank in v1 without schema migration
  • Dedup guard runs in-process per-request in MVP; migrates to a persistent vector index in v1
  • Supabase handles connection pooling; Railway/Fly.io backend scales by adding replicas

Data Flow

1
Upload: Teacher drops 3–5 PDFs → Next.js → POST /sessions/{id}/files → FastAPI saves to Supabase Storage → uploads each to Gemini Files API → caches gemini_file_uri in source_files table.
2
Blueprint: POST /sessions/{id}/blueprint → FastAPI calls extract_blueprint(file_uris) → Gemini returns BlueprintSchema JSON → stored in sessions.blueprint.
3
Generate (streaming): POST /sessions/{id}/generate/stream → generate_paper_stream() calls generate_content_stream → SSE events: start → chunk × N → section × sections → complete {paper}.
4
Edit / Regenerate: PATCH /sessions/{id}/paper for inline edits; POST /sessions/{id}/regenerate/question for Gemini-powered question swap; POST /sessions/{id}/regenerate/section for full-section replacement.
5
Answer Key: POST /sessions/{id}/answer-key → generate_answer_key(paper) → AnswerKeySchema returned and displayed alongside the paper.
6
Dedup: POST /sessions/{id}/dedup → check_duplicates(paper, source_pdfs) → embeddings compared via cosine similarity → DuplicateWarning[] with severity returned to UI.
7
Export: GET /sessions/{id}/export → FastAPI renders Jinja2 HTML template → returned to Next.js → browser print dialog → PDF.

AI Integration

Provider: Google Gemini via the google-genai Python SDK.

Models: gemini-2.5-pro for initial paper generation (most demanding reasoning task); gemini-2.5-flash for blueprint extraction, question regeneration, and answer key (speed-critical, lower cost); text-embedding-004 for duplicate-content embeddings.

Prompting strategy: Every AI call uses response_schema=<PydanticModel> via response_mime_type="application/json" — Gemini returns machine-validated JSON, not free text. Each module (blueprint.py, generate.py, regenerate.py, answer_key.py) maintains its own prompt template encoding explicit constraints: marks totals, question counts, marks-per-sub-part sums, no verbatim copying of source passages, difficulty distribution guidelines, and cultural relevance nudges.

Shared wrapper: ai/wrapper.py:call_structured() handles retries (max 3), post-validation callbacks, model fallback, and per-call UsageRecord logging. No AI module calls the SDK directly; all calls go through the wrapper.

Known Technical Limitations

  • Anonymous sessions only — session UUIDs provide no cryptographic isolation between users.
  • gemini-2.5-pro free-tier quota is 0 RPM; a paid key is required for the pro-model generation path.
  • Dedup embedding check runs per-request with no persistent index; large source corpora will slow this call.
  • Export uses the browser print dialog; a server-side headless PDF renderer is needed for bulk export.
  • Subject scope is A-Level English only; other subjects require prompt re-engineering.

Section 07
Security

Authentication & Authorisation

PaperForge uses anonymous sessions in this build — no login is required. A UUID4 session ID is generated on the user's first visit and stored in localStorage. All backend resources (source files, blueprints, generated papers) are scoped to the session_id column in every database table. Supabase Row-Level Security (RLS) policies are defined and ready; they are not enforced in MVP because there is no JWT to verify against, but the column structure means enabling auth is a one-line schema change for v1.

Data Handling

Uploaded PDFs are stored in a private Supabase Storage bucket; they are accessible only via signed URLs generated server-side. No PII is collected — teachers are fully anonymous. Session data (blueprints, generated papers) lives in Supabase PostgreSQL scoped to session_id. Gemini Files API stores uploaded PDFs for 48 hours and then auto-deletes them — no long-term storage on Google's side.

API & Secret Management

All secrets — GOOGLE_API_KEY, Supabase service key, DATABASE_URL — are stored exclusively in backend/.env, which is listed in .gitignore and has never been committed to the repository. The frontend uses only the Supabase publishable anon key (safe for client-side exposure by design). No secrets appear in the Next.js bundle or any environment variable prefixed NEXT_PUBLIC_ except the publishable key.

Input Validation

  • File type: Only application/pdf MIME type accepted at upload; non-PDF files are rejected with HTTP 422 before they touch storage.
  • File size: Configurable maximum (default 20 MB) enforced by FastAPI's request body limit.
  • AI output: All Gemini responses are validated against Pydantic schemas (BlueprintSchema, PaperSchema, AnswerKeySchema); malformed output triggers a retry rather than passing through to the UI.
  • SQL: All database access goes through SQLModel's ORM layer — no raw string interpolation; SQL injection is not possible via the application layer.
  • Session IDs: FastAPI validates that the session_id path parameter is a valid UUID4 before querying the database.

Known Vulnerabilities or Gaps

  • Session isolation is UUID-only — if a session ID is shared or guessed, another user can read that session's papers. Accepted for MVP hackathon use; fixed in v1 with JWT auth.
  • No rate limiting on API endpoints — acceptable for a controlled demo; required before public launch.
  • Gemini-generated content is not moderated for harmful material — acceptable for an internal school tool with trusted users.

Edge Cases

  • Corrupted PDF: FastAPI catches the PyMuPDF parse error and returns HTTP 422 with a message: "Could not read this PDF. Please check the file is not password-protected or corrupted."
  • Too-small PDF (<2 pages, <500 words): Blueprint extractor raises a post-validation error and tells the user the paper is too short to analyse reliably.
  • Marks drift >10%: generate.py post-validator retries the Gemini call; after 3 retries, returns the best-effort result with a visible warning in the UI.
  • Quota exhaustion (429): wrapper.py catches the error and retries with gemini-2.5-flash automatically; the teacher sees no interruption.
  • Mid-stream disconnect: SSE error event fires, UI shows "Generation interrupted — retry?" with a button that resumes from the last confirmed section.

Section 11
Why This Project Leads the Track

Technical Edge

PaperForge uses three distinct Gemini capabilities within a single coherent product: gemini-2.5-pro/flash for structured multimodal reasoning (blueprint extraction from PDFs, paper generation), gemini-2.5-flash for speed-critical sub-tasks (question regeneration, answer key), and text-embedding-004 for semantic duplicate detection. This is not a wrapper around a single Gemini chat call — it is a multi-model AI pipeline where each model is chosen for the right job.

  • Structured JSON at every AI boundary — every Gemini call uses response_schema=<PydanticModel>. No prompt parsing, no regex, no "hope the model formats it correctly". The output is machine-validated JSON or the call retries.
  • Production-grade SSE streaming — real-time paper generation via Python async generators through FastAPI's StreamingResponse to a Next.js EventSource. Section completion events fire as each section of the paper is completed, not as raw token chunks.
  • Self-rejecting AI pipeline — the backend post-validates every generated paper against marks constraints. If the total drifts more than 10%, it rejects its own output and retries. The system is opinionated about quality, not just speed.
  • pgvector-ready architecture — the duplicate guard uses text-embedding-004 + cosine similarity today and will migrate to a persistent cross-session vector index with zero schema changes in v1.

Problem-Solution Fit

The problem is real, specific, named, and quantified: A-Level teachers spend 4–8 hours per paper. Every feature in PaperForge traces directly to a step in that drafting process. Blueprint extraction replaces the manual structure-counting step. Constrained generation replaces the blank-page drafting step. Inline editing and per-question regeneration replace the revision step. Answer key generation replaces the marking-scheme-writing step. Duplicate detection replaces the manual cross-check step. The product does not add features for demo purposes — every feature exists because a teacher needed it.

Execution Quality

In 20 hours, the team shipped: a full-stack web application with a production FastAPI backend, a Next.js frontend with a multi-step wizard, 7 distinct REST and SSE endpoints, a shared AI wrapper with retry and fallback logic, 5 separate Gemini-integrated AI modules, Supabase PostgreSQL + Storage integration, and a Jinja2 PDF export pipeline. The entire AI layer is tested with sample scripts that can be run in isolation — the demo does not depend on live Gemini calls to look good.

Real-World Potential

This does not end at the hackathon. Three tutoring centres have already expressed interest in piloting. The architecture is designed for the next phase: pgvector is instrumented, Supabase RLS is ready for auth, and the prompt templates are parameterised by subject so adding Maths or Sciences is a configuration change, not a rewrite. The Cambridge A-Level cohort spans 130+ countries — the addressable market is genuinely large, and the team has direct distribution into the initial beachhead segment.
